# Relvor: pass 5, product identity

Passes 1–4 made Relvor calm and well proportioned. This pass makes it
recognisable. The rule was to impress through clarity, not decoration. Every
signature answers one of three questions: **what matters, what changed, what
should I do next.**

## The three signatures

These appear in Management, Staff and Client alike. See them together on the
Visual system page.

### 1. The Brief: "I understand my operation in ten seconds"

Every home opens with the operation written as a short paragraph, not a grid
of widgets:

> **4 sessions** run today across **2 locations**, 15:30 to 20:30, with
> **53 expected**. **2 need a decision** before tonight, and **4 approvals**
> are waiting. Everything else is on track.

- **Live facts are tokens.** Tokens are set on a soft tone and link straight
  to the thing they describe. Risk tokens are red; nothing else is coloured.
- **An amber live dot** leads a kicker that says when the brief was written.
- **"Since your last visit"** follows: two or three changes, each dated and
  linked. That is the "what changed" answer, and it's the same pattern in
  every area.
- **Each area gets its own voice:**
  - Management: the whole operation.
  - Staff: "You're coaching 2 sessions today… for U12 Academy, Charlie is
    marked unavailable, so expect a change."
  - Client: "Alfie's next session is today at 5:30pm… new feedback is ready
    to read."

### 2. The Day Line: the operation in time

- **What it shows:** the day as a horizontal line of hours. "Now" is marked
  in amber, elapsed time is hatched, and each session sits where it happens.
  Problems sit in time too; U12 Academy's staffing issue is visibly tonight.
- **Management:** every session across the organisation.
- **Staff:** your own sessions, with your next one outlined in amber.
- **Client:** the same idea at week scale, a seven-day line with today
  raised and marked in amber, past days faded.
- **On phones:** the line scrolls sideways and opens on the first thing
  still to come.

### 3. State marks and decisions: one attention language

- **Three marks, used everywhere:**
  - ring: to do;
  - diamond: warning;
  - filled dot: urgent.
- **Marks travel upward.** A row's mark shows on its index tab, and the
  worst mark inside a module shows beside it in the sidebar. So "Schedule &
  Sessions ●" is visible from any screen.
- **Urgent items become decisions.** They're no longer rows in a list:
  - a raised block marked "Decision needed", with its deadline;
  - a title and context;
  - the one action that resolves it, attached.
  Management Home shows them straight after the Day Line. Everything
  non-urgent drops to a calmer "Also on your list".

## Index tabs: section navigation

As you asked, sections are now boxed tabs, built so they don't look childish:
- **Two lines per tab:** the section name, plus a live status line with its
  state mark, for example "Staff · 8 people · 3 to check ◆" or "Today · 4
  sessions · 1 issue ●". Only the urgent part of the status line is red.
- **Unselected tabs** are quiet boxes, a shade lighter than the header band,
  with hairline edges on three sides.
- **The selected tab** becomes the page surface and opens into the content
  below, with soft inverted corners. It carries **the amber edge**, a 2px
  line along its top. That edge is Relvor's only decorative flourish, and it
  always means "you are here".
- **Section transitions:** choosing a tab settles the content in with a
  short 260ms rise. The motion is disabled when reduced motion is requested.

## Shell

- **Sidebar:** the organisation block now carries a live line ("● Live ·
  4 sessions today"), so the sidebar reads as a running system rather than a
  menu.
- **Module marks:** every module in the sidebar shows its worst state mark.
- **The amber accent** is kept for exactly four jobs:
  - Now;
  - the selected edge;
  - the live dot;
  - the primary action.
  Because it's scarce, it becomes the brand.

## Recognisable in two minutes

Within a couple of screens someone will have seen:
- a written Brief;
- an amber Now line through their day;
- the same three marks on rows, tabs and sidebar;
- decisions presented with their action;
- the amber edge on whichever tab is open.

None of these is a standard dashboard pattern, and all of them repeat.

## Unchanged

- **Structure:** routes, destinations, and the Manage/Staff switch.
- **Data:** every fact in the Briefs and change lists comes from the same
  mock data as before, with nothing new implied.
- **Visual base:** the pass-3 tokens and surfaces, and the pass-4 workspace
  header structure.
